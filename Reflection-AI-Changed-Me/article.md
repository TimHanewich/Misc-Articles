# Reflecting on how AI has Changed Me
It has been roughly three years that industries have been adapting to the newfound capability that is generative AI, first brought to the masses with the release of ChatGPT in late 2022. 

Only three years in, AI has *already* changed my daily life in many ways, affecting both my personal and professional life. I spent a number of years in the technology industry prior to the "AI wave" that we are in right now and also a number of years *within* the wave. My approach to how I work and produce has changed *so much* in *such a small amount of time*. 

I could go on and on and on about how much my life has changed since the dawn of generative AI, and evolved since then! But in this I want to share something more intimate: a reflection on how I have noticed AI changing the way I think. Increasing, decreasing, enhancing, and diminishing certain qualities.

These observations are difficult to make for a few reasons. For one, these changes in the past several years have been slow and gradual as AI has become a greater and greater influence in my daily life, with more and more delegation to it. But secondly, self reflection is challenging as it forces us to look very objectively at ourselves and construct a candid assessment of ourselves, for better or for worse.

Below I'll touch on a few areas that I feel I have noticed a tangible difference in. Regarding the degree that these characteristics changed, I am uncertain - at the end of the day, I'm still the same person. But these minor differences are enough for me to acknowledge.

## Cognitive Intelligence
I do not have any direct quantitative results to point to this - but I feel my raw *Cognitive* intelligence (my ability to reason and problem solve) **may have decreased**. 

In this era of AI, we have access to tools that are, in fact, not "tools" in the sense of what may conjur up when you think of "tool". These AI *tools* like [Claude Code](), [Cursor](), [Codex](), and [GitHub Copilot]() are harnesses that harness the raw intellectual power of the latest large language models. 

These agents themselves do not just have specific defined skills or capabilities. They "think". They "reason". They "problem solve". Assign any task you want to an AI agent through one of these harnesses and they will execute an agentic loop to read, understand, explore, test, execute. Along the way, when they encounter problems, they will troubleshoot and pivot to new novel techniques mid-stream. If you have used these, you will know what I mean.

WIth how powerful these models are in this agentic loop scenario (powered by a harness), and how strong they are at finding solutions to problems, you can truly delegate *so much* to them - not just the *doing*, but the idea making, architecture creating, solution drafting, and more.

As I've been more and more impressed with agents ability to reason and develop complex solutions to complex problems, I've (within reason), delegated more and more challenging tasks to it. And this works at any level: delegate something very specific and it'll do it! Delegate a higher level business problem and it will come up with ideas to solve for and then steps to solve - and then execute for you!

Cognitive Intelligence is like a muscle: if a human does not use their muscles, they will atrophy. While I don't have qualitiative evidence to suggest my intelligence has decreased (e.g. several before-AI and several after-AI IQ test results), I would not be surprised if it has given the fact that I am *not* using the cognitive "muscle" of deep thought and focus on deep logical problems.

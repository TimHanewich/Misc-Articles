## HOW DID WE GET HERE? CULTURE
Culture of western world puts onus on performance and productivity. Story of the fisherman

I have only done a small amount of world travels and have not immersed myself in various world cultures like many travel-hungry folks may have. But I am aware of the beast that is "Western Culture" that I have been raised in and how it may compare and contrast to other world cultures. Western culture's fixation on productivity has very deep roots that stems back many generation:
- Religions like Protestantism viewed work as a "saving grace" that may enable the common man to reach salvation
- The industrial revolution spawned the use of the time clock, using time as a metered instrument to produce.
- Management science in the 90s began seeing the labor force as a resource to optimize to achieve maximum productivity.

And, in terms of economic output, this has served the US economy well! One of the most successful and prosperous economies has been born from this productivity-focused culture, establishing the US as a superpower as only a relatively young nation compared to superpowers throughout history.

While there are many benefits associated with a prosperous economy (quality of life improvements, healthcare, safety, abundance of nutrition, etc.), this isn't to say that this does not come with side effects.

Identity begins to be tightly associated with what level of economic output you produce, your job, level of importance of your role. 

Young children as early as elementary school are taught to push for good grades so they can land in advanced placement classes in high school. Students in high school are pushed to score highly on placement exams to get to the best universities. Students in universities are pushed to acquire internships with Fortune 500 companies to better their odds of reaching a lucrative career. New hires out of university are encouraged to work long hours to get promotions. Those are promoted are encouraged to take leadership positions to have even greater impact. Those in leadership positions are encouraged to move further up the chain to lead entire organizations.

While this mindset has produced one of the most prosperous nations the world has ever seen, it has also very tightly tied the identity or worth of humans to what level of productivity and success they are capable of achieving as a result of their inmate abilities and skills they work hard to develop along the way. It becomes a sense of self worth for many.

## THE DISRUPTION: AI
Throughout the development of every economy since the dawn of civilization, the driving force was human effort: human labor and human intellect. As economics developed further, capital (machinery) has folded into the mix and automated a large part of what human labor was driving. This has led more and more to the role of humans in the modern economy moving towards information economy ("white collar") jobs in which intellectual output was the valuable resource contributed.

Since the dawn of man, human effort was the only top line force driving the world economy. This has now changed. Now, Artificial Intelligence has proven to be capable enough to directly integrate in our economy and serve in many of the roles or capacities that humans have today.

For the first time in history, critical thinking can be delegated to a machine. Human cognition is no longer the only source of intellect. 

This new form of intellect, delivered by AI, is faster, deeper, cheaper, and more widely available. In a capitalist economy, this will drive massive shifts as both the cost and speed of delivering intelligence is being drastically undercut in front of our eyes in real time. 

This will have massive implications for the economy and the future of the job market for human workers. There are economists much smarter than me that are (with the assistance of AI, probably!) forecasting what our economy in the coming decades may look like. In this post I don't want to pontificate on the future of the economy, but what this change will bring, or at least risks bringing, to the human spirit.

As I mentioned earlier, the western world has manifested a culture of productivity and intellectual achievement so heavily that this is often tied to a sense of self-worth and purpose. With the now abundance of great intelligence at a fraction of the cost and speed of human intellect, capitalist economies will soon do away with the very expensive and inefficient supply of human intellect. As this happens, millions upon millions of white collar workers across the globe will be separated from the very work that they have spent decades become proficient at and have grown a sense of identity in. This, for most, will be a very rough wakeup call.



## THE RISK THIS POSES

Many predict a future "utopia" in which AI is driving > 95% of the economy with work becoming optional for humans. Humans will receive Universal Basic Income (UBI) checks simply for existing, with those UBI checks being funded by both heavy taxation on the the economic transactions performed by AI and proceeds from a "soveign wealth fund" that represents ownership of AI infrastructure or capital for the masses.

While I'm sure there are many out there that love the sound of sitting back and watching their favorite show endlessly with a glass of their favorite red wine hand while getting paid to do nothing, the enjoyment of this would only be short lived. 

Zoo animals unhappy with everything free and provided. Depressed. But when you put them in the wild, where they struggle and have death around the corner, they thrive. 

The very first zoo was in 3500 BCE, discovered by archaelogisits in Egypt found remains of animals that appear to have been kept in capacity. And in the 5,000+ years since then, the psychological wellbeing of animals in captivity has been a serious problem for zoo operators!

[PICTURE OF DEPRESSED TIGER]

In a zoo environment, every possible need of an animal is provided to them with no required level of effort by the animal: food, shelter, healthcare, and protection. Captivity has entirely removed the challenges these animals face and must overcome by foraging, hunting, outrunning predators, etc. While it may seem this is a utopia perfect for any animal that would otherwise be facing the risks of the wild, it is truly far from it.

Many zoo animals in this "utopia" suffer a serious form of depression, boredom, and lack of control over their environment or situation by applying themselves. For many centuries, vetenaroans and animal behavioralists have studied this and put in place artificial challenges/threats to rectify the issue: puzzle feeders, hidden food, new objects, varried terrain, and stimulating activities. And these efforts have brought about modest improvements to the psyche of these animals in captivity. 

It is such an interesting aspect of the animal psyche that a "utopia" in which every possible need is supplied with no level of effort actually leads to less fulfillment than the environment where every meal must be fought for, an injury may mean death, and a predator is just around the corner ready to pounce, actually leads to greater fulfillment.

Something within these animals is wired to gain satisfaction and fulfillment from "the struggle" itself. And don't think this is limited to just animals - we as humans also have had this issue for decades on the physical side of our lives! The industrial revolution and years thereafter brought about the heavy use of machinery to perform the physical demands of the economy. As it has, our physical bodies have become weaker and weaker, atrophying. But we invented a solution! The gym: artificial physical work for the sake of satisfying our animalistic need of maintaining our physical bodies which also greatly influences our psychological happiness.

The intellectual information economy we have developed is our modern day society is the equivalent of the hunting, foraging,  shelter building, and all the other important things that fulfills a wild animals life. This has become our way of remaining intellectually active, and a sense of purpose and meaning in our life.

The invention of machinery removed the need for human physical labor. The invention of AI that reasons and thinks will remove the need for human intellectual cognition. We may be moving towards a future in which apply ourselves is going to become optional, with much of the rewards we have been working towards (money, housing, etc.) provided to us automatically in the form of universal basic income, produced by the gains of AI; a "zoo" of sorts.

## THE PROBLEM
Where will this leave us? In a Western world so heavily obsessed with producicity and self identity tied to intellectual output, being placed in "a zoo" in which you no longer have to apply yourself will be shattering for many. 

As AI grows more and more capable, and more and more widely applied, the skills that workers spent decades developing and look to as their job security and financial security will no longer be as valuable as they once were. This sudden shift will be something hard to reckon with - and something that no generation before us ever had to face.

Apart from "the what", described above, I've recently spent time pondering "the why"? Why does the absense of challenges, the absense of struggle, pose such an existential burden on us humans? Two reasons come to mind:
1. We are wired that way as animals. Evolution has "wired" the chemical receptors in our brain to be satisfied when we "do a good job". We face a challenge, solve a problem. It feels good.
2. In Western (and becoming global) culture, we have developed to tie our self-worth and identity on what we can intellectually produce. Our intelligence is a tool that allows accomplishmnent. And left with nothing to accomplish by our own faculties, we lose our sense of self-value.

This is already happening across many industries today.
